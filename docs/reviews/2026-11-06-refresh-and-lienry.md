# Lienry and refresh.tech: design review, 6 November 2026

## In short

refresh.tech has redesigned a lot since we copied its layout on 20 October. Most of the change is about
finish and calm. The content sits in narrower blocks with more air around it. Every page now ends the
same way. The FAQ is a quiet list instead of a stack of boxes. Pages show the product working instead
of only describing it. The footer looks like a finished sheet rather than one more band.

Lienry is already ahead in places refresh cannot match. Our software window is a live 3D model, where
theirs are pictures of screens. Our fit check gives a score on the page, where theirs is an embedded
form. We have a hands-on wash demo and a real booking calendar. Our phone layouts are sounder, and
everything we show is honest: no invented customers, logos, quotes or badges.

So the plan is to take refresh's new structure and polish where it helps us, keep what we do better,
and leave out anything that rests on customers, certifications or content we do not have. Five
designers will build it in parallel, each in their own part of the site, then the usual checks run on
every page before anything goes live.

## What refresh changed

**Across the site**
- **One ending for every page.** A short, centred panel in the accent colour, about 1000px wide
  instead of full width. It has a two-line statement and two choices: the main ask, plus a lighter
  step such as "try the tool".
- **A new FAQ.** The heading sits in its own left column. The questions run down the right in the
  heading typeface, separated by thin lines, with a small chevron. No boxes, no plus buttons.
- **The footer is a sheet.** Rounded top corners and a gentle dark wash, so the page ends on a finished
  object.
- **An announcement strip** above the header, for a dated live event.
- **New menus.** Each panel opens on a feature card (a product picture, a quote or the latest news).
  The panels have large group headings, and the page behind them dims and blurs while a menu is
  open. The phone menu is a tidier list.
- **A narrower measure.** Content now sits in about 1200px (1140 on product pages) while the header
  bar stays wide. The pages read noticeably calmer.
- **Italic payoffs.** Most section headings now end on an italic phrase in the accent, and the big
  headlines are set tighter.

**Product and solution pages**
- The platform page opens on the product's own screen in a soft glass frame, not on a photo.
- Each product module has its own page.
- Feature rows have become chapters. Each row sits on its own band, alternating white and a pale tint,
  with smaller pictures beside a narrower block of text.
- Small product cards sit over the photographs, so the photo shows the setting and the card shows the
  software at work.
- The security section is now a confident dark panel with four fact tiles.
- A row of four module cards near the foot of each page leads the reader on to the rest of the
  product.

**Home**
- The steps section is now one framed object: the film first, with the step tabs underneath it.
- The quiz card floats on a soft shadow, and its score sits on its own raised white card.
- The articles grid is six identical light cards. Each one has a clear action button, with the date
  on the right.
- The customer cards end on a full-width outline button.

**Company, tools and new pages**
- The About page is now a run of set pieces: a mission panel, a story with a clickable timeline, a
  dark manifesto card over a blurred photo, and tilted, framed photo cards.
- The readiness-check page has one title, a progress bar at the top of its card and a closing panel.
- New pages: case studies, an academy of courses and a resources hub.

**Where refresh slipped.** On phones the new two-column FAQ squeezes its heading until words break
in half. Its customer cards collapse into very narrow columns, and some italic headings fall back to
the wrong font. It also still puts small dotted labels above most headings. We will not copy any of
these.

## How the two sites compare today

### Home
- **Refresh does better:** its tools read as objects with space around them, where ours run the full
  width of the page. The steps section reads as one product. The articles grid scans in one pass and
  every card says what clicking it does. Its FAQ looks lighter. Its quiz card feels raised and
  tactile, where ours is flat lines.
- **Lienry does well:** the fit check already has the score out of 100, the three zones and the
  prompt, and it goes further with the live count, coloured answers, the points it raises and the
  sticky score on phones. The steps section is interactive (you can wash the facade yourself). The
  booking calendar is a real tool. The honest stand-ins for customer material hold up. The phone
  layouts are cleaner than refresh's.

### Platform and Lienry Desktop
- **Refresh does better:** it shows the product first. Ours opens on the same photo a visitor has
  just seen on the home page, and our best asset, the live software window, waits at the very bottom.
  Refresh also answers "will it fit, is it safe" on the page. Our /platform has no safety section,
  and /download ends without an ask.
- **Lienry does well:** the window is a working 3D model with honest demo labels. The part tabs are
  smooth and accessible, and every part has its own link. The copy is short and admits what is
  undecided.

### Solution pages (commercial, homes and rentals, solar)
- **Refresh does better:** a long page reads as chapters. Product cards on the photos show what the
  system does. Every ask comes with a lighter second option. The dark safety panel is a strong beat
  before the FAQ. Its body text is darker and easier to read than our grey.
- **Lienry does well:** live, interactive demos (the 3D window, the hands-on app). Careful citations.
  Pages that are shorter and faster. Headlines that never shift as the fonts load.

### Company and enquiries
- **Refresh does better:** the About page tells its story with a timeline and a change of pace. Ours
  repeats one picture-and-paragraph block four times, and on a phone it is a long run of text and
  pictures.
- **Lienry does well:** it says plainly where the company is (concept stage, nothing in service yet).
  The enquiry page and its form are well made, and the bubble hero already matches refresh's.

### Tools and legal
- **Refresh does better:** every tool page ends with a next step. Its tools share one look, and its
  readiness-check page has a single title. Its legal page fills the width, while ours leaves a third of
  the desktop page empty.
- **Lienry does well:** both tools work live on the page and say plainly that they are indicative.
  The policy is easy to read, and the 404 already offers two ways on.

### Header, menus and footer
- **Refresh does better:** every menu panel opens on a feature card, and the page dims behind an open
  menu. The phone menu keeps its groups, and the footer has a finished edge.
- **Lienry does well:** no labels above headings and no buttons in the header bar. The menu icons
  already match refresh's new style in our own drawings, and "Ask AI" is named in text rather than
  with other companies' logos. The motion is calm and rule-bound.

## What we will change

### Every page
- **One compact closing panel with two choices** on every product, solution, company and tool page.
  It is about 1000px wide and shorter than today's: the main ask as the white pill, plus a lighter text
  step (usually "Check your building"). *Why:* every page ends with a clear, confident next step, and
  undecided visitors get an easier one.
- **The new FAQ.** The heading on the left stays in view as you scroll. The questions sit on the
  right in our serif on thin lines, with a chevron. On phones it stacks, so nothing is squeezed.
  *Why:* it looks calmer and more editorial, and it takes ten boxes off every page.
- **A footer with rounded top corners.** *Why:* the page ends on a finished edge.

### Header and menus
- **A product card in the Platform menu.** It is accent blue, with a small coded map of the demo
  building labelled Demo data and a "See the platform" link.
- **A fit check card in the Solutions menu.** *Why:* the menus show the product and offer the next
  step, without any customer claim.
- **The page dims behind an open desktop menu.** *Why:* the open panel reads as the one layer.
- **A tidier phone menu.** Thin row dividers, the open section on a pale tint with its group names
  kept, and a link at the end into that section's main page. *Why:* phone visitors keep the structure
  desktop visitors see.

### Home
- **The fit check as a floating card.** It is narrower, raised on a soft shadow, with the score on
  its own white inset card and quieter answer buttons until you choose. The title moves beside the
  score, so the card is shorter. *Why:* it is the first thing under the hero, and it should look like
  a finished product.
- **The steps section as one framed instrument,** with the picture first and the five step tabs
  under it. *Why:* the stage and its progress read as one piece of software.
- **The "Inside the platform" grid as six matching light cards.** Each has its picture, a serif title,
  a clear action button ("See the system", "Try the tool") and its subject on the right. *Why:* it
  scans in one pass and every card says where it goes. No dates are invented.
- **The three "Built for the buildings you own" cards end on a full-width button,** with their
  outlines removed. *Why:* the action is obvious and the three feet line up.
- **Booking calendar fixes.** No empty gap under the times on phones, and open days drawn as filled
  circles. *Why:* it looks finished just as someone is about to book.

### Platform and Lienry Desktop
- **/platform opens on the live software window** in a soft glass frame, under a centred headline and
  its two buttons. *Why:* the product comes first, and visitors no longer see the home photo twice.
- **Two product cards over the /platform photos.** The pressure for each material, and the re-scan
  finding debris, both labelled Demo data. *Why:* the photos show the setting and the cards show the
  software at work.
- **Two new side-by-side panels on /platform.** One covers what the building provides (water, power,
  a desktop, the app). The other covers how it keeps people off ropes. *Why:* it is our honest answer
  to refresh's integrations and security panels.
- **/download gets the same hero frame and a closing panel.** *Why:* it no longer ends without an
  ask.

### Solution pages
- **Feature rows become chapters.** Each sits on its own band, alternating pale blue and white, with
  smaller, wider pictures beside the text. *Why:* a long page reads in steps instead of one white run.
- **Small Demo-data cards over the row pictures,** for example between cleans, the ground pod, and the
  row schedule. *Why:* every chapter shows the system working, not just the software row.
- **A second button in the hero,** to check your building or property. *Why:* visitors who are not
  ready to talk get a next step.
- **Safety as a dark panel with four fact tiles.** On /solar, the cited figures get the same panel.
  *Why:* a confident beat before the FAQ, and it replaces a soft, over-stretched photo.
- **Darker body text, neat square tick marks and one paragraph per row.** *Why:* the copy reads sure
  of itself, not faded.

### Company
- **The story becomes a pale-blue panel** with tilted, framed pictures and a clickable plan timeline:
  designed, raising now, building, the Sydney pilot, and the hose-free idea (marked as exploring). It
  has no dates and no amounts. *Why:* it gives the page a centrepiece and answers what investors and
  pilot buildings ask: where are you, and what comes next.
- **The pre-seed band centred,** with three short facts: what the round funds, who we want to hear
  from, and what we share. *Why:* our honest version of a "backed by" section.

### Tools and privacy
- **/fit-check has one title and a progress bar** at the top of the card. On phones the questions
  come first, and the score strip follows you down the page. *Why:* no doubled title, and phone
  visitors start answering straight away. This is easy to switch back if you prefer the home order.
- **The calculator moves onto the same tinted hero as the fit check,** so the two tools look like a
  set. Each tool page now ends on the closing panel, pointing at the other tool.
- **Tool FAQs that explain the tool.** How the score is worked out, what happens when you ask for your
  result, how the totals are added up, and that figures never leave the browser. *Why:* showing the
  method is the professional thing to do for an indicative tool.
- **The calculator keeps its totals in view on phones.** A one-line readout sits under the main field.
- **Privacy: an "In short" summary card** in the empty right-hand side of the page. *Why:* it fills
  the layout and answers "what happens to my details?" at a glance.

## What we are leaving out, and why
- **Customer stories, logos, testimonials, results, case studies and certification badges.** We have
  none, so the site keeps showing honest stand-ins.
- **An academy, an articles hub and dated news.** We have no articles or courses, so there is nothing
  honest to fill them with.
- **Other companies' AI logos in the footer.** We keep the text links.
- **Refresh's phone FAQ and its italic font slip.** These are their mistakes, not changes to copy.
- **Per-part pages such as /platform/scan.** Worth doing for the software-led parts later. The
  hardware parts should wait for your new Higgsfield imagery, so we do not repeat pictures.
- **Tighter headline spacing.** Good, but it means re-measuring every headline, so it is better as a
  small change of its own next time.

## Decisions for you
1. **The end of the home page.** Keep the booking calendar beside the FAQ (what we will do now), or move
   it to the enquiry page as a "Book a call" option and end home on the compact closing panel, as
   refresh now does?
2. **Italic payoffs in section headings.** Refresh ends most headings on an italic phrase. Today we keep
   the italic for each page's main headline (your 28 October polish). Shall we use it more widely, about
   one heading in three?
3. **A narrower page width** (about 1200px for content, with the header bar staying wide) across the
   whole site. This round only narrows the key panels. Going site-wide changes every page.
4. **An announcement strip above the header.** Only with a true, current message, such as the pilot
   intake, the pre-seed round or a Desktop release. Do you want one, and what should it say?
5. **A short vision statement signed by Liam** on /company, over a darkened photo. It needs words you
   approve to go under his name.
6. **Menu group headings in the serif.** Refresh uses large serif headings in its menus. We kept the
   menus all in Inter on 28 October. Change that?
7. **Privacy page wording:** the contact email (still open), and the line saying the policy should be
   reviewed by a legal adviser, which reads like a draft note on a live page.
